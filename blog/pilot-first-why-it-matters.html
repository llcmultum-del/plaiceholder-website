Why We Run Every Agent on 20 Records Before Anything Else

2026-11-05

Every agent we build runs on 20 real records before it touches anything else. This is non-negotiable, and it's not a formality. The pilot is the most important part of the build.

What the pilot is for

Building an agent and running it in production are two different things. In the build phase, we know the task, the tools, the safety rules. We've run it on sample data. We think we know what "good" looks like.

The pilot tests that assumption against your real data, with the people who will actually use the output.

Twenty records is enough to catch the things that generic testing misses: the edge cases in your specific CRM setup, the way your team describes things differently from the discovery interview, the signals that turn out not to matter as much as everyone thought.

What it typically catches

In nearly every pilot, we tune at least one thing before the full run. Common adjustments: the scoring rules weight something too heavily, the agent flags a type of record the team doesn't want to see, or a data field that was supposed to be reliable turns out not to be.

These are small changes — but they're the difference between an agent your team trusts and one they stop checking after a week.

Why we measure on the same 20 records

At handover, we time how long the task took before the agent and after, using the same 20 records. This gives you real, specific numbers — not an estimate, not a benchmark from someone else's deployment. Your data, your team's time, measured directly.

We only publish those numbers once you've reviewed and approved them. The pilot is also how we keep that standard honest.
